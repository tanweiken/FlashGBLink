**Experimental pre-release of the SO ready-line performance line.** Plugin
`0.3.0b1`; `main` stays on the 0.2.2 release.

The payload drives the link port's SO line — high while busy, low once the next
word is loaded — and the Pico clocks as soon as the GBA is ready instead of
waiting a fixed 150 µs per word.

## Speed

Measured on the tested 32 MiB `M36L0R8060B` bootleg cartridge with a 4 MiB
Apotris image at 1.25 Mbaud:

| Operation | 0.2.2 | 0.3.0b1 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| read 512 bytes | ~2 s | 0.07 s | ~28× |
| 4 MiB read | 160 s | 45 s | **3.6×** |
| 4 MiB verified write, CLI | 6:03 | 2:13 | **2.7×** |
| 4 MiB verified write, FlashGBX | 6:44 | 2:22 | **2.8×** |
| multiboot upload | 1.5 s | 1.8 s | unchanged (BIOS paced) |

## Install

All three parts have to come from this release — the payload, the plugin and the
firmware are a matched set:

1. **Flash the Pico** with `gbasend-pico.uf2` below (or `gbasend-pico-rgb.uf2`
   for a board with a WS2812 LED on GP16). Hold BOOTSEL, copy to `RPI-RP2`.
2. **Install the plugin**: extract `FlashGBLink-plugin.zip` below into
   FlashGBX's `plugins/` directory. It already contains the matching GBA
   payload.
3. **Build `gbasend`** from <https://github.com/tanweiken/gbasend-rs> at commit
   `a1257e4`. Upstream `nullstalgia/gbasend-rs` does not carry the handshake, so
   its nootloader and speed test do not match this firmware.

## Compatibility

- `main` (0.2.2) and upstream `gbasend` are unaffected; do not mix a payload
  from one line with firmware from the other.
- The handshake only engages at or above 1 Mbaud. The BIOS multiboot upload
  (256 kHz), a session opened below 1 Mbaud, and an older payload all keep the
  fixed delay, so mixed installs stay safe but get no speedup.
- Validated on the M36 cartridge above only; other profiles are not yet tested.
