**Experimental pre-release of the SO ready-line performance line, now defaulting
to the 1.8 Mbaud clock it was measured clean at.** Plugin `0.3.0b4`; `main` stays
on 0.2.3.

The payload, plugin and firmware from `0.3.0b3` are unchanged — the firmware is
still `tanweiken/gbasend-rs` `b68a37d`. What changed is the clock: `flash.py`
and the FlashGBX plugin now negotiate **1.8 Mbaud** instead of 1.25 Mbaud, the
fastest rate measured without a mismatch on the test link. 1.9 Mbaud mismatches,
so 1.8 is the top working setting rather than the middle of a range.

## Speed

Measured 2026-11-04 on the tested 32 MiB `M36L0R8060B` bootleg cartridge with a
4 MiB Apotris image, `b68a37d` firmware (5 µs arm / 20 µs settle):

| Operation | 0.2.2 baseline | 1.25 Mbaud | 1.8 Mbaud (new default) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 MiB read | 160 s | 58.4 s | **52.1 s** (78.6 KiB/s) |
| 4 MiB verified write, CLI | 6:03 | — | **1:54** |
| read per word | 150 µs fixed | 55.7 µs | 49.7 µs |

The 1.8 Mbaud read returned a byte-identical image to the 1.25 Mbaud one. The
wider handshake margins needed for these clocks cost about 13 µs per word at a
fixed clock; the higher clock gives back roughly half of that, and the write also
benefits from the CRC verify from `0.3.0b2`.

## Install

1. **Pico**: `gbasend-pico.uf2` below (or `gbasend-pico-rgb.uf2` for a WS2812 on
   GP16). If `0.3.0b3` is already flashed, the firmware is identical — no need to
   reflash.
2. **Plugin**: extract `FlashGBLink-plugin.zip` below into FlashGBX's `plugins/`
   directory.
3. **gbasend**: build from <https://github.com/tanweiken/gbasend-rs> at
   `b68a37d`.

## If 1.8 Mbaud is unstable on your setup

The link limit is per cable, GBA and cartridge; the test link only failed at
1.9 Mbaud, but a slower setup may top out earlier. Fall back without touching the
firmware:

- CLI: `flash.py --baud 1250000 ...`
- FlashGBX: lower the baud rate in the FlashGBX settings; the plugin clamps to
  it.

A mismatch shows up as a timeout or as data that fails its own CRC, not as a
silently accepted write, because the payload's CRC verify covers every
programmed sector.

## Compatibility

- `main` (0.2.3) and upstream `gbasend` are unaffected; do not mix a payload
  from one line with firmware from the other.
- The handshake only engages at or above 1 Mbaud. The BIOS multiboot upload
  (256 kHz), a session below 1 Mbaud and an older payload all keep the fixed
  delay, so mixed installs stay safe but get no speedup.
- Validated on the M36 cartridge above only; other profiles are not yet tested.

## Update: the 1.8 Mbaud default was a regression

Measured afterwards through FlashGBX on the same cartridge: 1.8 Mbaud with
the 5 µs / 20 µs margins gives 39–40 KiB/s, while `0.3.0b2` at 1.25 Mbaud
with the 2 µs / 5 µs margins gives 42–45 KiB/s. The wider settle costs more
per word than the higher clock recovers. `0.3.0b5` restores the narrow
margins and the 1.25 Mbaud default; until then, pass `--baud 1250000` or
lower the baud setting in FlashGBX.
